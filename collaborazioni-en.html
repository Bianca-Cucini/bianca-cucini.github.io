Tra Noi

Switzerland

  • Daria Spiridonova — violin
  • Silvia Berchtold — recorders
  • Bianca Cucini — viola da gamba
  • Rafaela Salgado — harpsichord

The early-music ensemble Tra Noi is known above all for its lively communication, among its members and with the audience: the "we" in the name is inclusive, embracing everyone present. Formed only in early 2023, it has already won the Konzerthaus Vienna Prize at the HIF-Biber Competition (2023), the Audience Award at the Biagio Marini Competition (2023) and at the Handel Competition Göttingen (2024), first prize at the Concours International de musique ancienne du Val de Loire (2024), third prize as a duo at the Van Wassenaer International Competition (2024), first prize and the Audience Award at the Orlando di Lasso Competition (2025), and the EUBO Award for "most promising ensemble" at the York Young Artist Competition. It has performed at venues including the Turin Chamber Music Festival, the Resonanzen festival at the Konzerthaus Vienna, the Telemann Society in Magdeburg, the Innsbruck Festival, the Cologne Philharmonic's FEL!X festival, and the Kaunas Philharmonic. Reviewed on Scherzo.ES.

Ensemble Zuléia

Switzerland

  • Léna Ruisz — violin
  • Zuguang Xiao — harpsichord
  • Bianca Cucini — viola da gamba

Ensemble Zuléia was formed in 2022 when its members met during their studies at the renowned Schola Cantorum Basiliensis, one of the leading institutions for early music, under the guidance of musicians such as Paolo Pandolfo, Leila Schayegh, Amandine Beyer, Jörg-Andreas Bötticher, Thomas Leininger and Edoardo Torbianelli. Its members, from China, Hungary and Italy, have explored a wide range of repertoire in Basel. In 2025 the ensemble won the prestigious Telemann Wettbewerb in Magdeburg.

Duo Cucini-González

Switzerland

  • Bianca Cucini — viola da gamba & quinton
  • Marinò González García — viola da gamba

Duo Cucini-González has performed together since 2025 in Switzerland (Basel), the Netherlands (The Hague), England (York Early Music Festival) and Italy (Florence). Formed under the guidance of Paolo Pandolfo, Mieneke van der Velden and François Joubert-Caillet, the two are noted emerging viola da gamba players on the international early-music scene.

Ensemble Rýnský

Czech Republic

  • Vojtěch Jakl — baroque violin
  • Elias Conrad — theorbo, baroque guitar
  • Bianca Cucini — viola da gamba
  • Irene González Roldán — harpsichord, organ

The award-winning ensemble Rýnský brings together outstanding musicians from an exciting young generation dedicated to early music. Its focus is on rediscovering the repertoire of the Stylus Phantasticus from Czech, German and Austrian archives. Founded during joint studies at the Schola Cantorum Basiliensis in Basel, and praised for its virtuosity, musicality and creativity, the ensemble regularly performs at renowned festivals and stages. It has been honoured with the International Manhattan Music Award (USA, 2023), the Audience Award of the Biagio Marini Competition Neuburg (DE, 2021) and the DV Young Sounds Award (IT, 2022), and is supported by the European S-Eeemerging programme. Its debut CD, devoted to the world premiere recording of all twelve violin sonatas by Ignazio Albertini, will be released on the DaVinci Records label.

Gli Incogniti

France

Gli Incogniti was founded in 2006 by a group of friends brought together by violinist Amandine Beyer, and takes its name from the Accademia degli Incogniti. Over the past twenty years the ensemble's core group of musicians has remained largely unchanged, while talented new members have gradually joined, bringing fresh ideas and renewed energy. The ensemble is regularly invited to the world's most prestigious venues and festivals. Bianca Cucini has performed with Gli Incogniti as solo viola da gamba player alongside Amandine Beyer herself, in concerts in Switzerland and Germany, at the Kölner Philharmonie.

Mare Nostrum

Italy

Founded in Rome in 2005 by Andrea De Carlo, Ensemble Mare Nostrum has established itself as one of the most distinctive voices on the European early-music scene. Their interpretative approach grows out of a deep exploration of the relationship between music, language, gesture and affect, restoring to baroque repertoire its full theatrical, physical and emotional dimension. Since 2012 the ensemble has focused on seventeenth-century Roman repertoire with The Stradella Project, a recording series devoted to the works of Alessandro Stradella: their most recent release, Un angelo del Paradiso (2024), received a nomination at the Gramophone Classical Music Awards 2025. Bianca Cucini collaborates with Ensemble Mare Nostrum.

Primary Colors

Switzerland

Primary Colors brings together a new generation of performers grounded in historical performance practice, with a focus on the lesser-known aesthetic practices of the 17th and 18th centuries. Combining wind, plucked and bowed instruments, the ensemble concentrates on German and Italian repertoire of the period, presenting works in instrumental combinations that would have felt familiar to their composers but are little known today. Founded in 2014 at the Schola Cantorum Basiliensis, it performs regularly in the Basel region. The ensemble has recently recorded a CD of virtuosic bassoon music by Georg Philipp Telemann, due for release in 2026.

The Little Light Consort

Switzerland

The Little Light Consort was founded in 2011 by viola da gamba player Soma Salat-Zakariás, with the aim of developing and bringing the historical and contemporary repertoire for viols to a wider audience. A shared musical understanding, gained through studies at the Schola Cantorum Basiliensis, provides a solid foundation for their interpretations. To refine the consort's performance practice, Salat-Zakariás, together with the Chest of Viols Association, initiated the building of a so-called "Chest of Viols": the six instruments, completed in March 2024, allow for an intense exploration of the repertoire and a seamless communication with the audience.

La Chimera

Italy

The ensemble, founded in 2001 by Sabina Colonna-Preti as a viola da gamba consort, took on a different, enriched form following the encounter with Eduardo Egüez: while preserving the original sound characteristics of the violas, La Chimera became a versatile ensemble made up of internationally renowned artists. The group's activity focuses on the creation of original projects in which the different art forms and cultures brought by its members converge — they come from Argentina, Venezuela, France, Senegal, Italy, Sweden, Cuba, Norway and beyond.
